Camera

On first start the browser will ask for camera permission, and the segmentation model (a few MB) will download once and cache in your browser. Once permission is granted the dropdown lists your available cameras, plus a "Raspberry Pi camera (network)" option that reads an MJPEG stream from the address above — picking a different camera restarts the feed on that one. The Pi option only works when running this page locally (via START_MIRROR_ME.bat), not on the https GitHub Pages link.

Second Camera (optional)

Off. Start the main camera first — it loads the detection models this feed reuses. This also needs a genuinely separate camera device; most desktop PCs only have one, and many webcam drivers refuse to open the same camera twice at once.

Camera & Refresh Settings

30 fps
6 / sec

Camera FPS asks your webcam to capture at that rate (actual rate depends on your camera/browser). Refresh rate controls how often the outline/gesture detection re-runs each second — lower it on slower computers, raise it for smoother tracking. "Accurate" body outline uses a much higher-quality segmentation model (~90MB one-time download, cached after) with cleaner edges, at the cost of a slower first load and more GPU use.

People

Have just that one person look at the camera, type their name above, then click "Learn This Person" — it needs a clear view of their face, not just their body. Register as many people as you like — each gets their own outline color. To improve someone already registered, type their exact name and click "Add More Samples" instead of re-learning from scratch. For the best results, click "Record Training Photos" and turn your head a little, try a few expressions, and step closer/farther for a while; it keeps capturing until you click "Stop Recording", then blends it all in. Recognition is also forgiving while you move: once it's confident who you are, it'll keep tracking you through a few frames where your face isn't clearly visible (turning away briefly, walking, being farther off) instead of needing a perfect match every single frame.

No one registered yet — everyone detected is outlined in plain red.

Registered people are live on the page as window.MirrorMePeople (e.g. window.MirrorMePeople["Rayden"] is true/false each frame, and window.MirrorMePeople._visible lists everyone currently matched), plus a mirrorme:person event on window with { name, active, similarity } — so you can assign different values or behavior per recognized person, including when two specific people are both in frame at once. Matching is based on lightweight on-device face geometry, not a production security system — very similar-looking people, extreme angles, or poor lighting can still confuse it.

Hand Gesture Training

Hold one hand clearly in frame, type a name above, then click "Record Gesture" and hold the pose still. To improve a gesture you already trained, type its exact name and click "Add More Samples" instead — that merges new captures into the existing one rather than replacing it.

Detected gesture: none

Trained gestures are live on the page as window.MirrorMeGestures (e.g. window.MirrorMeGestures["thumbs-up"] is true/false each frame), and as a mirrorme:gesture event on window with { name, active, similarity } whenever a gesture turns on or off — open devtools console to read it, or add your own script after this one to react to it in code.

All video, segmentation, face/hand tracking and "learning" happen locally in your browser using TensorFlow.js and MediaPipe. No frame, image, face or profile data is ever uploaded or sent anywhere — everything is only saved in this browser's local storage.
